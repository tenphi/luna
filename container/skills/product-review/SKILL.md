# Product research

Identify the exact model, revision, configuration, intended use, budget, and market. Use known user context; ask for a missing constraint only when it changes the choice. Do not assume a country or a household preference.

## Evidence that changes the decision

Read the relevant sources using available web or browser tools. Manufacturer pages establish specifications, support, warranty, and compatibility. Independent hands-on tests establish measured performance; owner reports can reveal recurring problems and longer-term experience. Separate these kinds of evidence.

Check the tested revision, publication date, test conditions, commercial disclosures, and sample size. A retailer rating or affiliate ranking alone does not establish quality. Look for consistent failures, repairability, spare parts, support lifetime, and current recall information when relevant. Explain credible disagreement and leave unsupported claims unknown.

Compare useful alternatives at equivalent configurations and current local prices. Include ongoing costs, returns, warranty coverage, and compatibility. The best choice can change with the user's needs or the price. Link material findings to the pages you actually read, with dates for prices and time-sensitive claims.

## Deliver the decision

Give a clear recommendation and confidence, the strongest reasons, meaningful drawbacks, and who should choose another option. Use a comparison table when it helps. Distinguish measured results, repeated owner reports, seller claims, and your inference. Avoid an artificial score or a certainty claim when evidence is thin.

When memory is enabled, look for the existing assessment page and preserve the complete review as a dated deliverable so later conversations can use it. Follow the knowledge base's placement and write policies; do not impose a personal folder layout or extract the review into disconnected conversational facts. Check the write receipt and read back the page. Without memory, provide the assessment in the conversation and do not claim durable storage.

When a measurement determines your recommendation, put a direct link to the measurement’s source in that paragraph. Links to a manufacturer or owner discussions cannot substantiate an independent lab result. Keep the measurement’s units, conditions and coverage limits beside the citation.
